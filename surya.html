Cook it right

The same rice, four ways.

Water ratios and timings that actually work for Surya — from a pressure cooker in Nagpur to a rice cooker in a hostel room.

Pressure cooker

The everyday north-Indian default. Works for both packs; black needs one extra whistle.

1 : 1¾
Rice : water
3

5 min
Rest
Pink JSR · Black HMT
01
Rinse twice, not five times

Two changes of water is enough to clear loose starch. Over-rinsing washes off the surface layer that gives Surya its aroma.

02
Soak 20 minutes

Cuts cooking time and helps every grain swell evenly rather than splitting at the ends.

03
Three whistles, medium flame

Add 1¾ cups water per cup of rice. Switch off at the third whistle — the residual pressure finishes the job.

04
Rest before you open

Five minutes with the lid on. Fluff with a fork, never a spoon, so the grains stay whole.

Timings are for one full whistle-free cycle on a medium flame. Adjust by a minute either way for your stove.

Open pot

How boiled rice is cooked across the south — drain the starch and the grains stay separate.

1 : 4
Rice : water
12–14 min
Boil
Yes
Drain
Black HMT · boiled
01
Bring water to a rolling boil first

Rice goes into boiling water, not cold. Four parts water per part rice gives it room to move.

02
Boil uncovered, 12 to 14 minutes

Test at twelve: the grain should crush between two fingers with a faint firmness in the middle.

03
Drain the starch water

Tip into a colander. Keep the kanji — it is the best thing to drink with a hot meal.

04
Steam-dry for two minutes

Return to the empty pot off the heat, lid ajar. The surface moisture leaves and the grains separate.

Timings are for one full whistle-free cycle on a medium flame. Adjust by a minute either way for your stove.

Rice cooker

For hostel rooms and small kitchens. Set it and forget it — the ratio does the work.

1 : 1½
Rice : water
Auto
Cycle
10 min

Pink JSR
01
Rinse in the inner pot itself

Two rinses, then level the rice with your palm so it cooks evenly across the base.

02
One and a half parts water

Less than a cooker needs — a rice cooker loses almost no steam, so extra water turns it sticky.

03
Let it switch to keep-warm

Do not lift the lid mid-cycle. The cooker knows when the water is gone.

04
Ten minutes on keep-warm

This is the step everyone skips. It finishes the centre of the grain.

Timings are for one full whistle-free cycle on a medium flame. Adjust by a minute either way for your stove.

Biryani & pulao

JSR holds its shape under layering and dum better than a long-grain basmati at twice the price.

Pink JSR
01
Soak a full 30 minutes

Longer soak, shorter boil. The grain elongates instead of breaking when you layer it.

02
Par-boil to seventy percent

In salted water with a bay leaf and cloves. The grain should still resist at the core.

03
Layer while hot, seal the pot

Rice over masala, saffron milk and fried onion on top. Seal with dough or a tight lid and a weight.

04
Dum on the lowest flame, 15 minutes

Use a tawa under the pot. Rest another ten minutes before you open it at the table.

Timings are for one full whistle-free cycle on a medium flame. Adjust by a minute either way for your stove.

Where it comes from

You can point at the mill on a map.

Surya is milled at our own plant on Vijayawada Road, Yadgarpally — not job-worked out, not blended from three traders. Paddy comes in from farmers in the surrounding mandals, gets dried, milled, graded and colour-sorted on the same premises, and leaves as a bag with a lot number on it.
